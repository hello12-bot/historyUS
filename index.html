A brief historical overview

History of the United States

From the many Indigenous nations who shaped this land to the diverse country it is today, explore key moments in America's past.

Explore the major eras and turning points that helped shape the United States.

Before European Colonization

For thousands of years, Indigenous peoples lived throughout North America. Hundreds of distinct nations developed their own governments, cultures, languages, and trading networks.

European Colonies

European countries established colonies in North America from the 1500s onward. English colonies grew along the Atlantic coast. Colonization brought the forced removal of Native peoples and the enslavement of Africans.

Independence and a New Government

In 1776, thirteen colonies declared independence from Great Britain. After the Revolutionary War, the Constitution established a new federal government. The country's founding promises of liberty and equality were not extended to everyone.

The Civil War and Reconstruction

From 1861 to 1865, the Union fought the Confederacy over secession and slavery. The Union victory ended slavery. Reconstruction amendments established citizenship and voting rights for Black men, although discrimination and racial violence continued.

Industry, Reform, and World Wars

In the late 1800s and early 1900s, industry and cities grew rapidly. The United States fought in both World War I and World War II. In 1920, the 19th Amendment prohibited denying the vote based on sex.

The Civil Rights Movement

During the 1950s and 1960s, civil rights activists challenged segregation and barriers to voting. Their work helped bring about major civil rights and voting rights laws. The movement built on generations of organizing for equality.

The United States Today

The United States is shaped by people from many backgrounds and by ongoing debates about rights, equality, and democracy. Its history continues to influence the country and its place in the world.
